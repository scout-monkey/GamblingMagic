What it really costs to pull a signed card

Magic's Star Trek set hides about 1,400 actor-signed cards inside its $37.99 collector boosters. Opening packs until you find one costs, on average, about $47,488. This page shows the math, then lets you try it with fake money.

The odds

Wizards of the Coast says a signed headliner card appears in fewer than 1% of collector boosters and that about 200 copies of each of the 7 cards exist. It doesn't publish the exact rate, so this page assumes one. Change it and every number below updates.

Why the chase feels winnable

You see the hits, never the misses

Pull videos show the one pack with the signature. The thousand packs that missed don't get posted.

A miss doesn't make you due

Every pack has the same odds as the last one. Opening 500 without a hit does nothing for pack 501.

Money already spent pulls you onward

After $2,000, stopping feels like wasting it. That money is gone either way, and the next pack costs the same as the first.

Small wins hide the loss

A $20 card from a $38 pack feels like a win. It keeps you opening while the running total goes down.

If you want the card

  1. Price the card itself first. For nearly every card in the set, buying the single costs a small fraction of what hunting for it costs. Run a hunt above and compare the spend to the card's price.
  2. Set the amount before you open anything. Treat it as the price of an evening's fun, the way you would a concert ticket, and stop when it's gone.
  3. Count the total, not the last pack. One good pull doesn't mean you're ahead. The profit or loss tile is the honest number.

If spending on packs has become hard to stop, the National Council on Problem Gambling runs a free US helpline at 1-800-GAMBLER.

What's assumed. The signed-card rate is this page's assumption, not a published figure. Card prices are TCGplayer Near Mint market prices from November 15, 2026.
